    Why the solos help

    The changes repeat every chorus for the whole tune, so a solo is not noise to discard — it is another pass over the same harmony. Confidence climbs the longer it listens, which is the opposite of a fingerprinter's fixed window.

    Stage 1

    Chroma, pooled by beat

    Bass and treble kept apart: pianists voice rootless, so the root is the bass player's. Pooling per beat makes tempo irrelevant.

    Stage 2

    Find the chorus length

    Self-similarity gives the form — 12 bars, 32, 48 — before any database is touched, and rules out tunes it cannot be.

    Stage 3

    Tune × key × position

    One circular correlation searches all three at once. Folding the query by the form length is what accumulates every chorus of evidence.

    Stage 4

    Then the head decides

    Twenty-eight tunes share the same twelve bars. Harmony can honestly say “a blues in F”; only the melody says which blues.
